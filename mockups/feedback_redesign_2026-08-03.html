One decision per screen, in the order a pupil actually thinks. First screen is the three doors; whichever door they pick becomes screen two; the how-to-do-it screen and the tomorrow question follow. Skip is always there and never nagged. Typical journey is two or three taps. The risk is that it can feel like a corridor when a pupil wants out, which is why every screen carries the same quiet skip and the whole thing tolerates being abandoned.

Everything on one screen, but held apart as three doors. The panels sit side by side and only the tapped one expands in place, so the pupil scans three headlines rather than a wall of prompts. The tomorrow question is pinned at the foot. Strength: nothing is hidden behind a step, one glance shows the whole offer.
